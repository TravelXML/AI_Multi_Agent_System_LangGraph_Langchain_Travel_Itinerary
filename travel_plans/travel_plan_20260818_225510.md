## 8️⃣ PRACTICAL TIPS

| **Safety** | Japan is one of the safest countries; keep passport and valuables in a hotel safe. |
| **Health** | No special vaccinations required. Bring any personal medication; pharmacies (ドラッグストア) are ubiquitous. |
| **Language** | Signs in major stations are bilingual (Japanese/English). Basic phrases: “Sumimasen” (excuse me), “Arigatō gozaimasu” (thank you), “Eigo o hanasemasu ka?” (Do you speak English?). |

## 8️⃣ PRACTICAL TIPS

| Topic | Advice |
|-------|--------|
| **JR Pass Activation** | Exchange your voucher at any major JR office (Narita, Tokyo, Osaka) **the first day you plan to use it**. Choose the 7‑day ordinary pass; it starts the day you activate. |
| **Internet** | Rent a pocket‑Wi‑Fi at the airport (≈ ¥400/day) or buy a data‑only SIM (¥3 000 for 7 days, 5 GB). |
| **Cash vs Card** | Japan is still cash‑friendly for small shops, temples, and street food. ATMs at 7‑Eleven, Lawson, and Japan Post accept foreign cards. Keep ¥10 000‑¥15 000 on hand. |
| **Luggage** | Most hotels/hostels allow luggage storage before check‑in/after checkout. Coin lockers at stations are ¥300‑¥700 per day. |
| **Etiquette** | Speak softly on trains, avoid eating while walking (except ice cream), remove shoes when entering traditional inns/temples. |
| **Safety** | Japan is one of the safest countries; keep passport and valuables in a hotel safe. |
| **Health** | No special vaccinations required. Bring any personal medication; pharmacies (ドラッグストア) are ubiquitous. |
| **Language** | Signs in major stations are bilingual (Japanese/English). Basic phrases: “Sumimasen” (excuse me), “Arigatō gozaimasu” (thank you), “Eigo o hanasemasu ka?” (Do you speak English?). |
