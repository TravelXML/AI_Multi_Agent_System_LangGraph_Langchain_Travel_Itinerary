## 6. Practical Tips  

| Topic | Advice |
|-------|--------|
| **Visa** | Indian passport holders need a **tourist visa**. Apply online via the Japan e‑Visa portal (https://www.visa.mofa.go.jp) – upload passport copy, photo, itinerary, and proof of funds (bank statement showing ≥ ₹ 1  lakh). Processing 4‑5 business days. |
| **Travel Insurance** | Look for policies covering overseas medical (≥ US $50,000), trip cancellation, and baggage loss. Providers like **Star Health, Bajaj Allianz, or TATA AIG** offer India‑based plans for ≈ ₹ 1,500‑₂,500 for a 5‑day trip. |
| **Money** | Japan is still largely cash‑friendly for small vendors. Load a **prepaid travel card** (e.g., Revolut, Wise) with JPY, or carry ~ ¥ 30,000 (≈ ₹ 18,000) in cash; the rest can be paid via card (most hotels, convenience stores, and larger restaurants accept Visa/Mastercard). |
| **Language** | Signage in major stations is bilingual (Japanese/English). Having a few phrases (“Sumimasen”, “Arigatō gozaimasu”, “Ikura desu ka?”) helps. Download **Google Translate** offline Japanese pack. |
| **Connectivity** | Rent a **pocket Wi‑Fi** at Narita (≈ ₹ 700/day) or buy a **data‑only SIM** (e.g., b-mobile, IIJmio) – ~₹ 1,500 for 5 days / 5 GB. |
| **Cultural etiquette** | Remove shoes when entering temples, ryokan, or some traditional restaurants. Speak softly on public transport. Tipping is not customary. |
| **Health** | No mandatory vaccinations for Japan from India. Carry any personal medication; Japanese pharmacies (薬局) are well‑stocked. |
| **Emergency numbers** | Police: 110, Fire/Ambulance: 119. English‑speaking helpline: 0120‑111‑000 (Japan National Tourism Organization). |
