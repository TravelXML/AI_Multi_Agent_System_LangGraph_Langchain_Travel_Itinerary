## 5. Booking & practical tips  

1. **Flights** – Set fare alerts on Google Flights/Skyscanner for DEL‑NRT and DEL‑HND. Aim to purchase by **early July 2026** (≈2½ months before departure) to lock in the ₹55k‑per‑person band.

## 5. Booking & practical tips  

1. **Flights** – Set fare alerts on Google Flights/Skyscanner for DEL‑NRT and DEL‑HND. Aim to purchase by **early July 2026** (≈2½ months before departure) to lock in the ₹55k‑per‑person band.  
2. **Hotel** – Reserve the APA Hotel via Booking.com or the hotel’s own site; free cancellation is usually available up to 24 h before check‑in.  
3. **IC Card** – Buy a Suica/Pasmo at Narita Express ticket office (¥2,000 deposit, refundable). Top‑up as needed; the card works on JR, private railways, subways, buses, and even convenience stores.  
4. **Hakone Free Pass** – Purchase online (Odakyu website) or at Shinjuku Station; it saves ~¥1,500 vs. buying each segment separately.  
5. **Weather** – Late September in Tokyo averages 22‑27 °C, with occasional rain. Pack light layers, a compact umbrella, and comfortable walking shoes.  
6. **Cash vs. Card** – Many small eateries and market stalls prefer cash (¥1,000‑¥5,000 notes). ATMs at 7‑Eleven, Lawson, and Post Office accept foreign cards.  
6. **Language** – Signage in major stations is bilingual (Japanese/English). Having a few basic phrases (e.g., “Sumimasen”, “Arigatō”) helps, but English is widely understood in tourist areas.
